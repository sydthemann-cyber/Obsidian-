## Presentation notes
#### Native range
eastern NA (Bama to NS, Canada, bounded on the west by MS river)
Wide tolerance of frost/temp/precip
Only moderate drought tolerance and elevation tolerance
E.g. no Q. rubra in peaks of Adirondack mts

#### Phenology
April: buds swell
April-may: leaf out, flowering
Catkins: clusters of flowers, male or female on same tree (monecious)
Aug-Oct: acorn shed
Leaf senescence: sept-Nov
Possibly changing due to ACC
Sticks in winter (deciduous)

#### Seed germ
Starts bearing after 20 yrs, peak after 40
Acorns: 2 season process
1: flowers spread pollen, germination/fertilization (fert takes about 1 yr to occur)
2: egg fertilization in late June, seed begins to develop, after 4 months: mature acorn

#### Acorns
See slide for acorn anatomy
Cotyledons: source of carbs for root/seedling dev, what the weevils + weevil larvae eat
Capule: attaches acorn to tree, protection for softer top
Seed coat: layer around embryo/cotyledons
Nat def: tannins, outer shell (fruit wall), capule
Need to balance to promote animal dispersal

#### Seedling + sapling
Cold stratification: acorns need to be in cold conditions for several weeks to start growing; otherwise they remain dormant
Seedling: Yr 1: large taproot from radicle, can grow up to a meter using cotyledon. Cycles btw growth + bud formation
Seedling: Yr 2-3: taproot becomes larger, storage root, lateral + fine roots for water + nutrient uptake
Oskar syndrome, periods of rapid growth in presence of sunlight; opportunistic growth for both seedling + sapling
Sapling: yrs 3-15: above ground growth

#### Mature trees 
65-100 ft tall
Alternate leafing pattern
Lobed (7-11), sinuses, bristletipped teeth
Red color during fall and on petioles

#### Acorn dispersal: 
falling down (too heavy to be blown), animals (esp. Rodents + birds) moving them
Rodents + birds cache them, can be forgotten -> become a tree
Less competition in further away place, dispersal! But needs to be good place for germination (in leaf litter/soil, not too much sun)

#### Masting:
Many trees across broad area release relatively large crop in 1 year
About every 2-5 years
Hypothesis: perhaps shared weather allows for such coordination, also defines the area of land sharing the same masting cycle
One study from Cali found shared weather to be a strong determinant of masting synchrony
Inter-tree synchronization, predator satiation hypothesis

#### Predation/Decay
Lifespan: 150-300 yrs, Q. rubra can be 400+
Main COD: environ stress
Insects (caterpillars, beetles, scales, etc), mammals (deer, mice squirrels etc), oak wilt (fungal disease) all factors
Stages of tree death:
Stressor, dieback, infestation + decay, death
10-50 yrs to fully decay
Dead oaks = important habitat/resource for ecosys
