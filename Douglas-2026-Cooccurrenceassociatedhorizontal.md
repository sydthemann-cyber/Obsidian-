###### Abstract
Understanding the drivers and consequences of horizontal gene transfer (HGT) is a key goal of microbial evolution research. Although co-occurring taxa have long been appreciated to undergo HGT more often, this association is confounded with other factors, most notably their phylogenetic relatedness. To disentangle these factors, we analyzed 15 339 marine prokaryotic genomes (mainly bacteria) and their distribution in the global ocean. We identified HGT events across these genomes and enrichments for functions previously shown to be prone to HGT. By mapping metagenomic reads from 1862 ocean samples to these genomes, we also identified co-occurrence patterns and environmental associations. Although we observed an expected negative association between HGT rates and phylogenetic distance, we only detected an association between co-occurrence and phylogenetic distance for closely related taxa. This observation refines the previously reported trend to closely related taxa, rather than a consistent pattern across all taxonomic levels, at least here within marine environments. In addition, we identified a significant association between co-occurrence and HGT, which remains even after controlling for phylogenetic distance and measured environmental variables. In a subset of samples with extended environmental data, we identified higher HGT levels associated with particle-attached prokaryotes and associations of varying directions with specific environmental variables, such as chlorophyll a and photosynthetically available radiation. Overall, our findings demonstrate the significant inf luence of ecological associations in shaping marine prokaryotic evolution through HGT.

### Summary

- analyzed 15,339 marine prokaryotic genomes and 1,862 ocean metagenomes
- tested whether microbial co-occurrence predicts HGT independently of phylogeny
- co-occurring taxa exhibited significantly higher HGT rates
- association remained after controlling for phylogenetic distance and environmental variables
- particle-associated microbes showed elevated HGT
- ecology is an independent driver of bacterial evolution through gene exchange

### Detailed Notes

#### Study design

- 15,339 high-quality marine genomes
- 1,862 ocean metagenomes
- HGT inferred using multiple complementary methods
- co-occurrence inferred from metagenomic abundance patterns
- controlled for:
  - phylogenetic distance
  - temperature
  - depth
  - chlorophyll
  - geographic distance
  - particle size fraction

---

#### Relationship between co-occurrence and HGT

- co-occurring taxa exchanged genes significantly more frequently
- co-occurrence remained a strong predictor after accounting for phylogeny
- ~9.6-fold increase in HGT odds for co-occurring taxa
- phylogenetic distance decreases HGT but does not explain observed patterns

---

#### Ecological mechanisms

##### increased transfer opportunity

- increased physical contact
- increased conjugation
- increased transformation
- increased phage-mediated transduction

##### shared selection

- organisms occupying similar niches experience similar selective pressures
- transferred genes are more likely to be adaptive
- adaptive genes are preferentially retained

---

#### Particle-associated communities

- elevated HGT
- increased local cell density
- prolonged cell-cell contact
- concentrated extracellular DNA
- increased phage interactions
- marine particles function as HGT hotspots

---

#### Major conclusions

- ecology directly shapes bacterial evolution
- HGT depends on encounter frequency as well as phylogenetic compatibility
- microbial community structure influences gene flow
