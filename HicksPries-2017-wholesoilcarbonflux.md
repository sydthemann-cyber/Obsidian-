###### Abstract
Soil organic carbon harbors three times as much carbon as Earth’s atmosphere, and its decomposition is a potentially large climate change feedback and major source of uncertainty in climate projections. The response of whole-soil profiles to warming has not been tested in situ. In a deep warming experiment in mineral soil, we found that CO2 production from all soil depths increased with 4°C warming; annual soil respiration increased by 34 to 37%. All depths responded to warming with similar temperature sensitivities, driven by decomposition of decadal-aged carbon. Whole-soil warming reveals a larger soil respiration response than many in situ experiments (most of which only warm the surface soil) and models.

### Summary

- Whole-soil warming (+4°C) increased **annual soil respiration by 34–37%**, substantially greater than estimates from most previous warming experiments.
- Soil carbon decomposition increased **throughout the entire 1 m soil profile**, demonstrating that subsoils contribute significantly to warming-induced carbon loss.
- Deep soils exhibited **similar temperature sensitivity (Q₁₀)** to surface soils, indicating that subsoil carbon is not inherently less responsive to warming.
- No evidence of **thermal acclimation** or substrate limitation was observed over the 27-month experiment.
- Warming primarily increased decomposition of **decadal-aged soil carbon**, rather than only recently fixed carbon.
- Current climate models likely **underestimate soil carbon-climate feedbacks** because most warming experiments only heat surface soils. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### Detailed Notes

#### Background

##### Soil Carbon

- soils store ~3000 Pg C
- contains ~3× more carbon than the atmosphere
- microbial decomposition returns CO₂ to the atmosphere
- warming may strengthen positive climate feedbacks

##### Knowledge Gap

Previous warming experiments

- primarily warmed upper 5–20 cm
- rarely included subsoils (>20 cm)
- potentially underestimated total carbon loss

---

#### Study Design

##### Objective

Determine how **whole-soil warming** affects

- soil respiration
- depth-specific carbon decomposition
- temperature sensitivity
- thermal acclimation

##### Experimental Design

Location

- temperate coniferous forest
- Alfisol soil

Treatment

- +4°C warming
- November 2013 – February 2016
- replicated field experiment

Heating system

- heating rods to 2.4 m depth
- surface heating cables
- warmed soil profile to 1 m depth

Measurements

- surface soil respiration
- CO₂ concentrations at multiple depths
- radiocarbon dating
- soil temperature
- soil moisture

---

#### Soil Respiration

##### Whole-Profile Response

+4°C warming

↓

34–37% increase in annual soil respiration

Surface measurements

- 1100 → 1450 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹

Profile estimates

- 1300 → 1750 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹

Increase substantially exceeded previous field warming studies.

---

##### Depth Contributions

Approximately

- 50% of total respiration originated below 15 cm
- 20% originated below 30 cm

Deep soils therefore contribute substantially to ecosystem carbon flux.

---

#### Temperature Sensitivity

##### Q₁₀

Mean apparent Q₁₀

- ~2.4–2.7

Meaning

- respiration approximately doubles for every 10°C warming

##### Response with Depth

All soil depths

- increased respiration
- exhibited similar Q₁₀ values

Conclusion

- subsoil carbon is similarly temperature sensitive as surface carbon

---

#### Thermal Acclimation

##### Observations

Over 27 months

- no decline in Q₁₀
- no reduction in warming response

Implications

- microbes did not acclimate
- substrate limitation not detected

Continued warming

↓

continued elevated respiration
