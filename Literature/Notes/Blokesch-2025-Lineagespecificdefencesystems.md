###### Abstract
### Summary

- Pandemic *V. cholerae* lineages encode distinct **mobile genetic element (MGE)-associated defense systems**, producing lineage-specific phage resistance.
- Phage resistance is largely mediated by **intracellular defense systems**, rather than receptor modification, preserving virulence traits.
- Major lineage-specific defenses include **PLEs**, **SXT ICEs**, **WonAB**, **CRISPR-Cas**, and **Nezha**, each targeting different stages of phage infection.
- ICP1 has evolved numerous **anti-defense mechanisms**, illustrating an ongoing evolutionary arms race.
- Defense systems likely influence **cholera epidemiology**, transmission dynamics, and the success of individual pandemic lineages.
- MGEs function as major reservoirs of bacterial immunity in *V. cholerae*, in addition to carrying virulence and antibiotic resistance genes. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### Detailed Notes

#### Phage Defense in Pandemic *Vibrio cholerae*

##### General Principles

- phages are major drivers of *V. cholerae* evolution
- defense systems differ between pandemic lineages
- many defenses encoded on **MGEs**
- receptor modification generally avoided because receptors often contribute to virulence
- intracellular defense preserves pathogenic potential while preventing phage replication

##### Major Defense Elements

7PET lineage

- **PLEs**
- **SXT ICEs**
- **WonAB** (WASA lineage)
- **DarTG**
- core genomic island defenses (reviewed separately)

Classical lineage

- **CRISPR-Cas**
- **Nezha**
- **Gabija**

---

#### Phage-Inducible Chromosomal Island-like Elements (PLEs)

##### General Characteristics

- integrated mobile genetic elements
- ~18–19 kb
- highly diverse (11 known variants)
- primarily defend against **ICP1**
- distributed patchily among 7PET strains

##### Infection Cycle

Following ICP1 infection

- excise from chromosome
- replicate
- hijack ICP1 structural proteins
- package themselves into phage-derived particles
- abolish ICP1 production
- horizontally transfer to new cells

##### Anti-ICP1 Mechanisms

**NixI**

- sequence-specific nicking endonuclease
- blocks transition to rolling-circle replication
- inhibits phage genome amplification

**TcaP**

- alters capsid assembly
- redirects capsid proteins into smaller procapsids
- prevents packaging of full ICP1 genome

**Gpi**

- inhibits ICP1 large terminase (**TerL**)
- blocks genome packaging

**LidI**

- disrupts lysis inhibition
- accelerates host lysis
- limits phage progeny production

**Rta (PLE11)**

- inhibits tail assembly
- functions even after CRISPR-mediated degradation of PLE DNA

##### ICP1 Counter-Defenses

**Adi**

- exploits PLE integrase
- destroys excised PLE DNA

**Odn**

- cleaves PLE origin of replication

**ICP1 CRISPR-Cas**

- broad-spectrum degradation of PLE genomes
- adapts through spacer acquisition

##### Evolution

- continual coevolution between ICP1 and PLEs
- modular evolution generates diverse PLE variants
- PLE variants appear and disappear over time
- likely fitness cost associated with PLE carriage

#### SXT Integrative and Conjugative Elements (SXT ICEs)

##### General Features

- self-transmissible conjugative elements
- originally recognized for antibiotic resistance
- later shown to encode phage defenses
- common in wave 2 and wave 3 strains

##### Organization

- conserved conjugation core
- variable cargo genes
- hotspot 5 consistently encodes phage defense systems

##### Defense Systems

**BREX**

- encoded by ICEVchInd5
- ICEVchBan9
- ICEVchInd4
- epigenetic restriction mechanism

**Restriction-Modification Systems**

- ICEVchInd6

**DISARM**

- ICEVchBan11

##### Biological Roles

- broad-spectrum phage resistance
- protect against multiple ICP phages
- transferable between bacteria

##### ICP1 Counter-Defense

**OrbA**

- anti-BREX protein
- neutralizes ICEVchInd5 defense

Additional escape

- epigenetic modification of phage DNA

##### Ecological Importance

ICP1 infection

→ stimulates SXT conjugation

→ spreads

- antibiotic resistance
- phage defense

simultaneously

#### WASA-1 Prophage

##### Distribution

- characteristic of wave 1 WASA lineage
- protects against broad range of ICP1 isolates

##### WonAB System

**wonAB operon**

- necessary and sufficient for defense
- active in both *V. cholerae* and *E. coli*

**WonA**

- ABC ATPase
- predicted sensor

**WonB**

- PD-(D/E)xK nuclease
- predicted effector

##### Mechanism

- abortive infection
- growth inhibition after infection
- prevents phage replication
- essentially no infectious particles produced
- ICP1 escape mutants not identified

##### Significance

- likely contributed to success of WASA lineage
- homologs present throughout Gram-negative bacteria

---

#### Classical Lineage Defense Systems

##### CRISPR-Cas

- type I-E
- absent from 7PET strains
- present in classical and environmental isolates
- experimentally demonstrated to provide phage immunity

Interesting observation

- transferable into 7PET
- remains functional
- reason for natural absence in 7PET unknown

---

##### Nezha

Components

**Sir2 NADase**

**HerA helicase**

Mechanism

- depletes intracellular NAD+
- blocks phage replication
- strong protection against ICP1

##### ICP1 Counter-Defense

**NrsA**

- ADP-ribose pyrophosphate synthetase

**NrsB**

- nicotinamide ADPR-transferase

Together

- regenerate NAD+
- restore phage replication
- overcome Nezha immunity

---

##### Gabija

- encoded alongside Nezha
- predicted phage defense
- secondary contributor compared to Nezha

---

#### Evolutionary Arms Race

##### Bacterial Adaptations

- acquisition of MGEs
- diversification of defense systems
- modular evolution
- horizontal transfer

##### Phage Adaptations

- anti-BREX proteins
- CRISPR-Cas
- Adi
- Odn
- NrsAB
- anti-DarTG proteins

Result

continuous defense ↔ counter-defense evolution

---

#### Epidemiological Significance

- phage resistance influences lineage success
- likely contributes to outbreak dynamics
- may determine persistence of specific pandemic waves
- defense systems represent major selective pressures during transmission
- comparative genomics links defense repertoires with global spread of pandemic lineages
