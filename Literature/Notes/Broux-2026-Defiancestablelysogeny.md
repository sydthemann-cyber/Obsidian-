###### Abstract
Stable prophage establishment is considered a quintessential response of temperate phages when faced with host cell depletion. Scrutinizing this behavior in model phage λ, however, revealed that many λ-chromosomes avoid stable integration in the host chromosome, and rather assume a nonintegrated prophage-like (or pλ) state inside the cell that keeps expressing CI-based immunity and becomes asymmetrically segregated. This creates a heterogeneous population of host cells that are either i) immune by stably carrying an integrated λ prophage, ii) immune by carrying a pλ-episome, or iii) λ-free and transiently immune by segregating from a pλ-carrier. Superinfection of these immune cells drains the number of λ-virions and creates more pλ-episomes that prolong the immunity and expansion of the λ-free subpopulation. The subsequent decline in λ-virions causes cytoplasmically inherited CI-levels in λ-free siblings to dilute out, poising them for renewed lytic consumption by λ. These overlooked pλ-dynamics attenuate the importance of prophage establishment and permit a more productive phage–host coexistence.

### Summary

- discovered a previously unrecognized infection state of bacteriophage λ termed the **prophage-like λ (pλ) state**
- under high MOI, many λ genomes do **not** integrate into the chromosome despite entering the lysogenic pathway
- instead, λ persists as a non-replicating episome that segregates asymmetrically during cell division
- pλ generates transiently immune, λ-free daughter cells that later become susceptible to reinfection
- these dynamics prolong phage production and promote long-term phage-host coexistence
- challenges the classical view that host scarcity primarily favors stable lysogeny

### Detailed Notes

#### Classical λ infection model

##### Low MOI

- favors lytic development
- phage replication
- host lysis
- horizontal transmission

##### High MOI

- high CII accumulation
- activation of:
  - cI
  - int
  - antiQ
- integrase inserts λ into chromosome
- CI represses lytic genes
- stable lysogen established

---

#### Discovery of the pλ state

##### Characteristics

- λ chromosome remains episomal
- does not integrate into chromosome
- does not replicate
- continues expressing CI
- maintains lysogenic immunity
- segregates asymmetrically during cell division

##### Consequences

daughter cells become heterogeneous

- true lysogens
- pλ-containing cells
- λ-free but transiently immune siblings

---

#### Transient immunity

##### Mechanism

- λ-free daughter cells inherit cytoplasmic CI protein
- inherited CI temporarily represses lytic genes
- immunity is gradually diluted during growth
- once CI concentration falls below threshold:
  - cells become susceptible again
  - reinfection occurs
  - productive lytic infection resumes

#### Ecological significance

- stable lysogeny is not the only response to host scarcity
- pλ maintains susceptible hosts within the population
- prolongs opportunities for horizontal transmission
- prevents rapid exhaustion of host population
- demonstrates infection outcomes exist on a continuum rather than a strict lytic/lysogenic switch

---

#### Experimental approach

- fluorescent ParB/parS chromosome tracking
- CI-responsive fluorescent reporter
- time-lapse fluorescence microscopy
- single-cell lineage tracking
- chromosome localization imaging
- high MOI infections

---

#### Major conclusions

- λ frequently avoids stable integration
- non-integrated prophage states can dominate high MOI infections
- asymmetric segregation creates phenotypic heterogeneity
- transient immunity fundamentally alters temperate phage ecology
- canonical λ life cycle underestimates infection complexity
