###### Abstract
Plasmids are key vectors for disseminating antibiotic resistance genes, yet their diversity and dynamics in the healthy human gut microbiome remain largely unexplored. Using fecal metagenomes from two cohorts (n = 498 samples), we constructed a comprehensive atlas of the healthy human gut plasmidome. We observed a polarization: while 97.4% of 19 151 plasmid clusters exhibited low prevalence (<5%), we identified 17 plasmid clusters that were detected in >30% of individuals. Among these, the plasmid pGut1 emerged as a paradigm of a stealth vector. Prevalent globally (>50% in independent cohorts), pGut1 possesses a minimal 4-kb conserved backbone ensuring stability and a hypervariable region acting as a “plug-and-play” module. We documented 40 distinct cargo inserts, including multiple antibiotic resistance genes such as cfr(C), erm(B), and aphA, across individuals, within individuals over time, and even within single fecal samples- validated by single-cell and long-read Nanopore sequencing. Screening of 2.3 million bacterial genomes revealed pGut1 in 93 strains across 49 genera and 2 phyla, including pathogenic Clostridioides difficile and three distinct Salmonella enterica strains. This pattern suggests potential repeated cross-species transmission events, equipping diverse pathogens with new antibiotic resistance genes. Our study exposes a hidden reservoir of highly adaptable, multi-host plasmids like pGut1 silently propagating antibiotic resistance genes in healthy populations. These plasmids, pre-adapted for cross-boundary dissemination, may pose a threat by fueling the emergence of multidrug-resistant pathogens.

### Summary

- characterized the healthy human gut plasmidome using 498 fecal metagenomes
- identified over 23,000 plasmid clusters, with >93% representing previously undescribed plasmids
- discovered a highly prevalent "stealth plasmid" (pGut1) present in >96% of one cohort and >50% of an independent cohort
- pGut1 possesses a conserved backbone and highly variable cargo region that frequently acquires antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes
- pGut1 was identified across 49 bacterial genera spanning two phyla, demonstrating exceptional host range
- healthy human populations represent an unrecognized reservoir of mobile AMR plasmids capable of dissemination into pathogens

### Detailed Notes

#### Healthy gut plasmidome

- analyzed 498 fecal metagenomes
  - cross-sectional cohort (258)
  - longitudinal cohort (240)
- identified:
  - 23,360 plasmid clusters (PLCs)
- only 6.4% matched known plasmids
- majority of gut plasmid diversity remains uncharacterized

---

#### Plasmid prevalence

##### Rare plasmids

- 97.4% of plasmid clusters
- detected in <5% of individuals
- likely transient or host-specific

##### Stealth plasmids

- 17 plasmid clusters
- detected in >30% of individuals
- persistent across time
- previously overlooked due to metagenomic limitations

---

#### pGut1

##### Distribution

- most abundant stealth plasmid
- detected in:
  - 96.5% of Chinese cohort
  - 53.4% of Human Microbiome Project cohort
- globally distributed

---

##### Backbone

Highly conserved (~4 kb)

Encodes

- replication protein
- recombinase
- YafQ-RelB toxin-antitoxin system

Backbone remained nearly identical across individuals.

---

##### Variable region

Acts as a modular "plug-and-play" cassette.

Contains

- 40 distinct cargo insertions
- extensive structural diversity
- rapid gene turnover

Cargo differs

- between individuals
- within individuals over time
- even within the same fecal sample

---

#### Antimicrobial resistance

Variable region frequently carried ARGs.

Examples

- erm(B)
- cfr(C)
- aphA
- lnu(C)
- tet(40)

Different pGut1 molecules carried different resistance genes while maintaining the same conserved backbone.

---

#### Host range

pGut1 identified in

- 93 bacterial genomes
- 49 genera
- 53 bacterial species
- 2 phyla

Included pathogens such as

- Clostridioides difficile
- Salmonella enterica

Demonstrates repeated cross-species transmission.

---

#### Evolutionary strategy

##### Conserved backbone

Maintains

- replication
- plasmid stability
- inheritance

##### Flexible cargo

Allows rapid acquisition or replacement of

- resistance genes
- accessory genes

Separates plasmid maintenance from adaptive evolution.

---

#### Biological significance

Healthy microbiomes contain

- persistent plasmid reservoirs
- extensive cryptic MGE diversity
- mobile ARG pools

Healthy individuals therefore function as reservoirs for future pathogen evolution.

---

#### Major conclusions

- gut plasmid diversity is substantially underestimated
- prevalent plasmids are evolutionarily distinct from rare plasmids
- pGut1 is a highly adaptable multi-host plasmid
- conserved backbone + modular cargo promotes rapid adaptation
- healthy human microbiomes contribute to dissemination of antimicrobial resistance
