###### Abstract
Cholera is a life-threatening infectious disease that remains an important public health issue in several low and middle-income countries. In 1992, a newly identified O139 Vibrio cholerae temporarily displaced the O1 serogroup. No study has been able to answer why the potential eighth cholera pandemic (8CP) causing V. cholerae O139 emerged so successfully and then died out. We conducted a genomic study, including 330 O139 isolates, covering emergence of the serogroup in 1992 through to 2015. We noted two key genomic evolutionary changes that may have been responsible for the disappearance of genetically distinct but temporally overlapping waves (A-C) of O139. Firstly, as the waves progressed, a switch from a homogenous toxin genotype in wave-A to heterogeneous genotypes. Secondly, a gradual loss of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) with the progression of waves. We hypothesize that these two changes contributed to the eventual epidemiological decline of O139.

### Summary

- Sequenced **330 O139 genomes (1992–2015)** to determine why *V. cholerae* O139 failed to establish the eighth cholera pandemic.
- Epidemic O139 originated **once** from a **wave-2 7PET O1 El Tor ancestor**, rather than multiple independent epidemic lineages.
- O139 diversified into **three temporally overlapping transmission waves (A–C)** with distinct evolutionary trajectories.
- Two major genomic changes accompanied the decline of O139:
  - progressive **loss of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes**
  - increasing **ctxB diversity** from a homogeneous to heterogeneous toxin genotype
- O139 lacked much of the **nan-nag region of VPI-2**, reducing sialic acid utilization and likely decreasing intestinal colonization fitness.
- The continued evolution of highly resistant O1 7PET strains likely outcompeted O139, preventing establishment of an eighth pandemic. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

#### Conclusions

- epidemic O139 originated from a **single 7PET O1 ancestor**
- three successive transmission waves evolved after emergence
- loss of AMR and increasing toxin diversity accompanied epidemiological decline
- incomplete **VPI-2** likely reduced host colonization efficiency
- continued evolution of highly fit O1 lineages ultimately displaced O139

---

#### Knowledge Gaps

##### Unknowns

- why serogroup switching produced only one successful O139 lineage
- ecological pressures driving progressive AMR loss
- fitness costs associated with different **ctxB** variants
- likelihood of future emergence of novel epidemic serogroups from the 7PET backbone
