### Summary

This paper investigates the mutations that arise when *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* evolves resistance to the type IV pilus (T4P)-targeting lytic phage **PO4**. Exposure to phage selected mutations throughout the T4P assembly and regulatory machinery, most of which abolished twitching motility and prevented productive phage infection. A novel **PilD duplication** delayed prepilin processing, leading to accumulation of unprocessed pilins that activated **PilSR-mediated negative feedback**, reducing pilA expression and functional pilus assembly. Unlike most resistance mutations, the PilD duplication frequently reverted through slipped-strand mispairing after phage removal, restoring both twitching motility and phage susceptibility. The study demonstrates that phages can act as powerful genetic probes for identifying functional regions of virulence systems while highlighting that the evolutionary stability of phage resistance depends on mutation type rather than simply the affected gene. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### Detailed Notes

#### Background

- T4P mediate
  - twitching motility
  - biofilm formation
  - surface attachment
  - host colonization
  - DNA uptake
  - phage adsorption
- PO4 is a lytic phiKMV-like phage that uses T4P as its primary receptor
- productive infection requires **pilus retraction**

---

#### Experimental design

- host = *P. aeruginosa* PAO1
- phage = PO4
- isolated 128 independent phage-resistant mutants
- whole-genome sequencing of all isolates
- characterized
  - twitching motility
  - surface piliation
  - pilin processing
  - cross-resistance
  - mutation reversibility

---

#### Major findings

- identified **28 unique mutations**
- mutations occurred in **16 T4P-associated genes**
- resistance mutations affected every major T4P subcomplex
  - pilus
  - motor
  - alignment complex
  - secretin
  - PilSR regulatory system
- nearly every resistant isolate lost twitching motility
- only three mutants retained detectable surface pili

---

#### Resistance mechanisms

Most mutations caused

- loss of pilus assembly
- reduced pilus function
- impaired pilus retraction
- decreased phage adsorption

Resistance did **not** always require complete loss of pili.

---

#### PilD

PilD functions

- prepilin peptidase
- N-methyltransferase
- processes T4P pilins
- processes T2SS pseudopilins

Novel mutation

- 12 bp duplication
- duplicates residues F184–V187
- located outside catalytic residues

Effects

- delayed prepilin cleavage
- accumulation of uncleaved pilins
- reduced extracellular pili
- impaired T2SS secretion
- loss of twitching

---

#### PilSR feedback regulation

Delayed PilD processing

↓

uncleaved prepilins accumulate in membrane

↓

PilS senses accumulated prepilins

↓

PilR activity decreases

↓

pilA transcription reduced

↓

smaller intracellular pilin pool

↓

less functional pilus assembly

↓

phage resistance

Shows that resistance results from both

- defective processing
- regulatory feedback

---

#### PilS N323A

Feedback-insensitive PilS mutant

Effects

- maintains PilR activation
- restores pilA transcription
- increases pilus production
- restores twitching
- restores phage susceptibility

Demonstrates PilSR regulation is a major contributor to the resistance phenotype.

---

#### PilA overexpression

Increasing pilA expression from a plasmid

- restored twitching
- increased pili

Did **not**

- restore phage susceptibility

Indicates that correct PilSR regulation is more important than simply increasing PilA abundance.

---

#### PilB

PilB

- extension ATPase
- powers pilus assembly

Mutation

- D388A
- adjacent to Walker B ATPase motif

Phenotype

- normal protein levels
- reduced surface pili
- no twitching
- resistant to PO4
- resistant to DMS3
- resistant to JBD68

Acts as a dominant-negative allele.

---

#### PilT

Frameshift mutation

Effects

- pili remain on cell surface
- no pilus retraction
- phage resistance

Confirms that **pilus retraction is required for infection**.

---

#### Cross-resistance

PilB D388A mutants resistant to

- PO4
- DMS3
- JBD68

Suggests multiple T4P phages rely on common aspects of pilus function.

---

#### PilD structure-function relationship

Insertion size determines function

- +1 aa → nearly WT
- +2–4 aa → impaired processing
- impaired T2SS
- phage resistance

Shows PilD is highly sensitive to structural perturbations outside its catalytic site.

---

#### Reversion

Mutation types tested

- missense
- nonsense
- deletion
- duplication

Only the PilD duplication reverted.

Mechanism

- slipped-strand mispairing
- duplication removed
- WT PilD restored
- twitching restored
- phage susceptibility restored

Codon-modified duplication

- no reversion

Confirms sequence duplication enables rapid evolutionary reversal.

---

#### Resistant isolates lacking detectable mutations

Some resistant isolates

- no detectable genomic mutations
- remained non-twitching
- remained phage resistant after prolonged passage

Suggests additional resistance mechanisms remain unidentified.

---

#### Phage therapy implications

T4P-targeting phages

- select against virulence
- reduce twitching
- reduce biofilm formation
- reduce host colonization

However

- some resistance mutations rapidly revert
- mutation stability should be considered when designing phage cocktails
