###### Abstract
Fitness tradeoffs between different environments enable the maintenance of microbial diversity. While the importance of tradeoffs is clear, it has been surprisingly difficult to predict which traits they will occur between and at how granular a level. **For example, it is unclear whether performance between a constant versus pulsed exposure of the same stress tends to be positively correlated, independent of each other, or negatively correlated.** Empirically, it has been shown that a critical feature structuring microbial communities is temperature. However, the compatibility between strategies to deal with different forms of heat stress is unclear. For instance, are strains that grow well at higher temperatures also stronger at withstanding heat shock? To understand how environmental microbes can adapt to better deal with heat stress, we performed an evolution experiment using a dominant phyllosphere microbe Methylobacterium extorquens in a regime of intermittent heat shock. W**e identified the genetic basis of adaptation, discovering a large number of loci capable of mediating adaptation to heat shock, many of which had not been previously linked to heat stress.** Despite the genetic divergence among evolved isolates, **we discovered a general tradeoff between heat shock resistance and growth at consistently elevated temperatures**. We found this tradeoff was not limited to evolved isolates, **but also represented across a sample of environmentally isolated Methylobacterium strains**. These findings indicate a generic conflict between strategies to deal with heat shock recovery and growth at elevated temperatures, **suggesting even variation in intensities of a stressor can drive diversity in microbial strategies.**

#### Intro
- relationship between stressor and tradeoff understood - relationship between fluctuating vs static stressor / intensity vs duration not understood
- increased phenotypic plasticity possibly 
	- niche expansion not always possible 
- dif temperatures favor different strategies -> diversification 
- 2 extremes - constant high temp vs short lethal temp + recovery at optimal temp (= heat shock)
- study of heat shock usually in pathogenic microbes 
- low understanding of pleiotropic effects + virulence 

effect of both high temp and heat shock
- increased reactive oxygen species ROS 
	- ROS removal helps alleviate heat damage
- protein misfolding 
	- upregulation of heat shock proteins and chaperones
	- global stress response 
- overexpression of heat shock proteins --> adaptation to growth at high temp and heat shock resistance 
- eg tradeoff 
	- loss of function mutations in chaperone dnaJ improves heat shock resistance - but slows high temp growth in salmonella and e coli 

heat shock
- increased lag time in survivors 
- divergent adaptive outcomes for fitness by reducing recovery time after heat shock 

#### Results 
##### heat shock leads to an increase in lag time
- short exposure - low loss of viability 
- transient heat stress - long lag, longer then explained by cell death 
	- damage vs physiological response 
##### evolution to heat shock selected for diverse beneficial mutations
- some parallelism but overall different targets of adaptation 
	- prev study found highly convergent adaptations 
- secA, dnaJ, hsp20 - no mutations in isolate A1 (???)

##### evolved isolated exhibit reduced lag after heat shock 
- imporvement in lag time - increased survivorship vs increase in recovery time for survivors
	- some isolates have higher survivorship, but doesn't account for all of imrpovement 
- none of the isolates had improved growth rate or lag in control treatment - advantage heat shock specific 
- A1 had NO mutations but was fitter then ancestor (????)
	- epigenetic adaptation 
- also one mutant with similar growth to ancestor - possible mutant only helpful in context of other mutated genotypes and not in isolation 
##### general tradeoff between heat shock recovery and continuous growth at elevated temperature for evolved isolates
- isolates with higher fitness in heat shock / high heat did worse in inverse 
- not locus specific - generic tradeoff 
- secA and dnaJ mutations most improved heat shock and worse at high heat
##### natural isolates also display a general tradeoff between heat shock recovery and continuous growth at elevated temperatures 
- also found in environmental isolates
