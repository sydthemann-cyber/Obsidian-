###### Abstract
Biofilms are structured multicellular bacterial communities encased within an extracellular matrix comprised of exopolysaccharides, proteins, extracellular DNA (eDNA), and other biopolymers that provide protection against environmental stressors. We and others have shown that eDNA serves as a fundamental structural element common to even multispecies biofilms. During biofilm maturation, ubiquitous DNABII proteins facilitate the conversion and stabilization of eDNA into the rare and rigid Z-DNA conformation, thereby enhancing matrix integrity and rendering the underlying eDNA resistant to nucleases. We have previously shown that the removal of positively charged molecules, such as DNABII proteins, results in rapid, significant disruption of diverse biofilms. Here, we identify the polyamine spermidine as another essential positively charged molecule that, together with DNABII proteins, contributes to the development and maintenance of the eDNA-dependent extracellular matrix. We also provide evidence that SPD is present within the biofilm matrix alongside DNABII proteins in multiple bacterial pathogens. Our findings indicate that SPD and DNABII proteins cooperate to promote Z-DNA formation. Depletion of SPD and DNABII using cation exchanger P11-phosphocellulose or inhibition of SPD synthesis via dicyclohexylamine impaired biofilm formation and destabilized preformed biofilms. These results suggest that polyamine synthesis or accumulation represents a potential target for biofilm disruption and control.

### Summary

This paper identifies **spermidine (SPD)** as a conserved structural component of the **eDNA-dependent biofilm matrix** across diverse Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. SPD cooperates with **DNABII proteins (HU/IHF)** to promote conversion of extracellular **B-DNA → Z-DNA**, stabilizing the biofilm matrix and increasing resistance to DNase degradation. Removal of SPD or DNABII proteins destabilizes biofilms, while inhibition of SPD biosynthesis prevents biofilm formation without affecting planktonic growth. The study proposes **polyamine metabolism** as a promising antibiofilm target. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

#### Polyamines

Major bacterial polyamines

- spermidine (SPD)
- putrescine (PUT)
- cadaverine (CAD)

Functions

- neutralize DNA phosphate backbone
- condense DNA
- regulate nucleic acid structure
- protect DNA
- regulate transcription
- oxidative stress protection

DNA affinity

SPM > SPD > PUT ≈ CAD

Only SPD efficiently promotes B→Z DNA transition in bacteria.

#### Spermidine is a biofilm matrix component

SPD detected in biofilms of

- *Haemophilus influenzae*
- UPEC
- *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*
- *Streptococcus mutans*
- *Klebsiella pneumoniae*

SPD

- accumulates as biofilms mature
- co-localizes with HU
- parallels accumulation of Z-DNA
- present throughout extracellular matrix

Suggests SPD is a conserved EPS component.

#### Z-DNA mechanism

SPD

- neutralizes DNA backbone
- facilitates left-handed Z-DNA formation

HU

- bends DNA
- stabilizes Z-DNA

Together

- stronger Z-DNA induction
- increased DNase resistance
- enhanced extracellular matrix stability

#### P11 phosphocellulose

P11

- cation exchange resin
- DNA mimetic
- binds positively charged molecules

Effects

- rapidly removes HU
- removes SPD
- prevents biofilm formation
- disrupts mature biofilms
- works within ~10 minutes
- no effect on planktonic growth

Mechanism

P11

↓

depletes SPD + HU

↓

less Z-DNA

↓

matrix collapse

Biofilm rescue requires

- SPD
- HU

simultaneously.

#### Cadaverine

CAD

- inhibits biofilm formation
- disrupts mature biofilms
- reduces Z-DNA accumulation
- reduces steady-state HU
- reduces SPD

Does not efficiently induce Z-DNA.

Likely competes with SPD-mediated DNA interactions.

Biofilm rescue again requires

- SPD
- HU together

#### Spermidine synthesis
