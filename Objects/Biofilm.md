### Summary
Biofilm development is a staged transition from planktonic attachment to mature, structured communities and eventual dispersal. Early attachment is mediated by flagella and type IV pili, then matrix production stabilizes the surface-associated state. During maturation, cells proliferate within an extracellular matrix, producing clonal clustering, spatial segregation, diffusion gradients, and increased stress tolerance. Dispersal is the regulated exit from mature biofilms back to motile life.
- **Attachment**: reversible surface interactions become irreversible adhesion as matrix accumulates.
- **Maturation**: multicellular architecture develops, with emergent ecological properties such as metabolic heterogeneity and competitive ability.
- **Dispersal**: cells leave the biofilm in response to lowered c-di-GMP, matrix degradation, pilus retraction, nutrient limitation, quorum signals, or stress.

## Detailed notes

### Matrix

The biofilm matrix is the structural scaffold of biofilms and is made of extracellular polymeric substances, including polysaccharides, proteins, lipids, and extracellular DNA (eDNA). Polysaccharides such as Pel, Psl, and VPS promote cohesion; eDNA contributes to mechanical stability, density, and genetic exchange; matrix proteins such as RbmA, RbmC, and Bap1 mediate adhesion and architecture; and outer membrane vesicles can package enzymes, proteins, and DNA.

---

### Regulation

Biofilm formation is controlled by regulatory networks that integrate intracellular signaling and population communication. c-di-GMP is central: it is synthesized from GTP by diguanylate cyclases, and high levels promote EPS secretion and surface attachment, while degradation by phosphodiesterases lowers c-di-GMP and promotes dispersal. Quorum sensing integrates cell density with biofilm behavior, including AHL-based regulation in Pseudomonas and RNA-based control in Vibrio cholerae through hapR. In Vibrio cholerae, vpsR and vpsT activate matrix genes, while hapR represses them at high cell density.

### Species-specific examples
- **Pseudomonas aeruginosa**
    - Pel polysaccharide and eDNA support matrix structure.
    - AHL quorum sensing regulates Pel and c-di-GMP synthesis.
- **Vibrio cholerae**

- VPS is the major matrix polysaccharide.
- RbmA, RbmC, and Bap1 provide architectural roles.
- vps production is tightly regulated because it is energetically costly.
- VpsO and VpsU provide tyrosine phosphoregulation of vps operons.

#### VPS assembly

Biofilm matrix production requires sequential VPS biosynthesis through a Wzx/Wzy-dependent pathway.

Matrix architecture depends not only on VPS quantity but also glycine and acetyl modifications introduced by VpsH and VpsG.

#### Environmental determinants of architecture

Carbon substrate chemistry is an ecological determinant of biofilm organization.

Even under identical physical conditions, changing substrate type from diffusible sugars to polymeric carbohydrates reorganizes community structure, species localization, and biofilm morphology.

#### Host-associated biofilms differ from abiotic biofilms

Biofilm formation within host mucus relies less on classical abiotic biofilm determinants (e.g., Fsr, GelE, sortase adhesins) and more on metabolic adaptation, stress sensing, and membrane glycolipid synthesis.

Host-derived physical structure can therefore be a major determinant of biofilm architecture.
