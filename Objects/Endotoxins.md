### summary
endotoxins are toxic lipopolysaccharides (lps) embedded in the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria. unlike exotoxins, they are not secreted proteins but structural components released in toxic amounts when cells lyse.

#### structure + biology
- composed of **lps** with:  
  - membrane-distal *O-specific polysaccharide*  
  - membrane-proximal *core oligosaccharide*  
  - **lipid A** (toxic phosphoglycolipid anchor)
- **lipid A** → primary toxic determinant  
- **polysaccharide region** → confers solubility and immunogenicity  
- both components required for host toxicity  

---

#### biosynthesis + variation
- lipid A synthesis highly conserved  
- enzyme modifications alter phosphate groups and fatty acid composition  
- variation influences host cytokine response and immune activation  

---

#### host effects
- triggers cytokine release (endogenous pyrogens) → fever  
- promotes diarrhea, toxic shock, inflammation  
- activates complement and coagulation cascades  
- decreases lymphocyte and platelet counts  
- generally less potent than exotoxins but causes systemic inflammation upon lysis
