### Summary

The Type IV pilus (T4P) machinery is a multiprotein nanomachine responsible for pilus biogenesis, extension, retraction, regulation, and prepilin maturation. Together these proteins control twitching motility, surface attachment, biofilm development, DNA uptake, and susceptibility to pilus-targeting bacteriophages. Mutations affecting different components produce distinct defects in pilus assembly while frequently converging on loss of twitching motility and phage resistance.

### PilA

#### Function

- major pilin subunit
- polymerizes to form the pilus filament
- substrate for PilD processing
- expression regulated by PilSR

#### Regulation

During impaired pilus assembly

↓

unprocessed prepilins accumulate

↓

PilS suppresses PilR

↓

reduced `pilA` transcription

↓

limits additional prepilin production

#### Experimental observations

- plasmid-based `pilA` overexpression restored twitching in PilD mutants
- increased PilA abundance alone did **not** restore full phage susceptibility
- productive infection depends on functional pilus assembly rather than pilin abundance

### PilB

#### Function

- extension ATPase
- powers pilus polymerization

#### Phage resistance

PilB D388A

- adjacent to Walker B ATPase motif
- stable protein
- reduced surface pili
- abolished twitching
- resistant to
  - PO4
  - DMS3
  - JBD68
- dominant-negative allele

#### Biological significance

- partial reduction of extension ATPase activity is sufficient for broad resistance to pilus-targeting phages
- complete loss of pili is not required

### PilD

#### Function

- prepilin peptidase
- N-methyltransferase
- processes T4P prepilins
- processes T2SS pseudopilins

#### Phage resistance

12 bp duplication

↓

delayed prepilin processing

↓

uncleaved prepilins accumulate

↓

PilSR negative feedback activated

↓

reduced `pilA` transcription

↓

decreased mature PilA

↓

reduced surface piliation

↓

loss of twitching

↓

phage resistance

#### Structure-function relationship

- insertion outside catalytic residues
- +1 aa insertion retained near-WT activity
- +2–3 aa insertions abolished processing
- +4 aa duplication caused delayed processing

#### Evolution

- tandem duplication frequently reverted after phage removal
- reversion mediated by slipped-strand mispairing
- restoration recovered
  - twitching
  - phage susceptibility

### PilT

#### Function

- retraction ATPase
- powers pilus depolymerization
- required for twitching motility

#### Phage infection

- PilT mutants retain abundant surface pili
- no twitching
- resistant to pilus-targeting phages

#### Biological significance

Pilus retraction transports pilus-bound phages toward the cell surface.

Surface pili alone are insufficient for productive infection.

#### Phage-selected resistance

- T4P-targeting phage PO4 selected mutations across nearly every T4P structural and regulatory subcomplex
- resistance did not always require complete loss of surface pili
- all resistant isolates tested lost twitching motility
- resistance mechanisms included
  - impaired pilus assembly
  - reduced pilin processing
  - reduced pilin expression
  - loss of pilus retraction
- T4P-targeting phages may select for reduced virulence because T4P contribute to
  - twitching motility
  - biofilm development
  - attachment
  - host colonization
