**What is the definition of infant mortality rate and why is it not a true incidence rate?**
- The definition of infant mortality rate is the risk of dying before 1 year of age, this is not an incident rate but rather cumulative incidence. Infant mortality is a measure of infant deaths within a population, and thus the denominator is # initially at risk. In order to be a true incidence rate, the infant mortality rate would have to take into account person time at risk (how long each baby lived prior to death), and the denominator would be total person time at risk.

# 25 
**In under 4 sentences compare and contrast how the South African Public Health Authorities might use HIV incidence and prevalence data in practice.**

Incidence is a measure of the rate at which new cases occur, while prevalence is a measure of current cases at a point in time. Incidence can thus be used for predictive purposes - such as planning how many HIV clinics or ART programs may be needed in the new year based on the number of expected new cases. In contrast prevalence could be used for mortality, or to better understand the actual disease burden in the population (even though the rate at which new diseases occur can be decreasing, the rate of people *currently* with the disease can still be increasing in a population).
