### summary
c-di-gmp (bis-(3'-5')-cyclic dimeric guanosine monophosphate) is a bacterial second messenger that regulates the transition between motile and sessile lifestyles. high levels promote Biofilm formation and surface attachment, while low levels enhance motility.

## detailed notes
### synthesis + degradation
- synthesized by **diguanylate cyclase** (enzymes; DGCs)  
- degraded by **phosphodiesterases** (enzymes; PDEs)  
- intracellular balance determines Biofilm vs planktonic state  

---

### functional roles
- promotes sessile growth and biofilm matrix production  
- represses motility and flagellar activity  
- activates transcription factors vpsR and vpsT  
- regulates adhesion and surface attachment through mshE  

---

### v.c. surface attachment
- **MshE** → ATPase receptor protein that binds c-di-gmp (Kd ≈ 1.9 ± 1.4 µM)  
  - binding promotes pilus extension and surface attachment  
  - low c-di-gmp enhances pilus retraction  
- **PilT** → retraction ATPase controlling near-surface motility  
  - required for type IV pilus function; works with PilU  
- **MshA** → structural pilin polymerized during attachment  

---

### regulation + interactions
- high c-di-gmp levels displace h-ns-dna complexes, activating Biofilm genes  
- feedback mechanisms integrate c-di-gmp with quorum sensing and stress response pathways
