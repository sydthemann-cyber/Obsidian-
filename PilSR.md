### Summary

PilSR is a two-component regulatory system that controls expression of the major type IV pilin gene, `pilA`, in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. It links the abundance and processing state of inner-membrane pilins to continued pilin production, coordinating gene expression with T4P assembly.

### Components

#### PilS

- inner-membrane sensor kinase
- responds to the abundance of membrane-associated pilins
- can act as a phosphatase toward PilR
- accumulated unprocessed pilins increase PilS-mediated repression

#### PilR

- response regulator
- phosphorylated PilR activates `pilA` transcription
- reduced PilR activity lowers pilin production

### Regulatory mechanism

Functional PilD

↓

prepilins processed efficiently

↓

mature PilA incorporated into pili

↓

inner-membrane pilin pool remains regulated

↓

PilR supports `pilA` expression

Impaired PilD

↓

unprocessed prepilins accumulate

↓

PilS inhibits PilR

↓

`pilA` transcription decreases

↓

mature pilin availability declines

↓

pilus assembly and twitching decrease

↓

T4P-phage resistance

### PilS N323A

- feedback-insensitive PilS variant
- deficient in PilR dephosphorylation
- maintains PilR activity despite pilin accumulation
- increases PilA production in a PilD-defective background
- restores
  - surface piliation
  - twitching motility
  - susceptibility to T4P-targeting phages

### Biological roles

- T4P homeostasis
- twitching motility
- surface attachment
- biofilm development
- phage susceptibility
- virulence regulation
