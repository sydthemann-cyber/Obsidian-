# ABSTRACT/SUMMARY OF PROPOSAL 2144 / 1750 

Prepare an executive summary detailing the what, where, and why of your proposed project․ If you are proposing the pursuit of a graduate degree program, summarize the program and its relevance to your career/education plans․ *

Links to websites are not permitted

I propose to complete the two-year Master in Biology at Universite Grenobles Alpes, beginning with the M1 in Molecular and Cellular Biology and continuing into the M2 in Microbiology, Infectious Diseases and Immunology. My undergraduate research has focused on how bacteriophages--viruses that infect bacteria--shape the growth and spatial organization of bacterial biofilms. My time at Grenobles will allow me to build a deeper microbiology, cell biology, biochemistry, and immunology foundation in order to further explore these interactions across scales.

The program combines rigorous coursework with sustained hands-on training and work experience during the internship period of both the M1 and M2 years. During M1 I will complete a semester-long research project and an internship, and in M2 I will undertake a six-month long internship in an academic or private laboratory. These experiences will provide me with a variety of research settings, and facilitate greater independence in experimental design, data analysis, and scientific communication. As a double major in Biology and Anthropology, I hope to expand on my previous fieldwork in Kenya on medicinal plants used by the Daasanach agropastoral community in Grenoble's laboratory in microbiology, structural biology, plant biology, and immunology.

After completing my master’s degree, I plan to pursue an MD/PhD with clinical training in immunology and epidemiology and a research focus on microbial interactions and infectious disease. A master’s degree within the French academic system would provide a strong foundation for further international training, while Grenoble’s broad curriculum would help me refine the questions and methodologies that will shape my doctoral research. Ultimately, I hope to translate research on phage–bacteria interactions into innovative clinical approaches to antibiotic-resistant infections. Achieving this goal will require collaboration among scientists with diverse expertise across the biological sciences, and Grenoble’s large, multidisciplinary research community offers an ideal environment in which to build these essential connections.

# FLEXIBILITY 2078 / 2000

Please explain how you will adapt to working and living in a new environment in the host country.

- Consider the ways in which you have grown throughout your life that make you the individual you are today.
    
- How have you demonstrated these qualities in your academic, professional, or personal life?
    
- Use specific examples from your personal experiences
    

2000 characters

I have learned to adapt to unfamiliar environments through discipline, resourcefulness, patience, and curiosity. As a high school rower, 4:45 a.m. wake-ups and ninety-minute practices taught me to build routines that sustain me under pressure. That discipline later carried me through 5:00 a.m. departures for field research in the Kenyan desert and helped me balance rigorous coursework with exploration of local beaches and mountains during a semester at the University of Auckland. Even relaxing pursuits, like teaching myself to play the guitar, require discipline and the commitment to stick to a schedule despite very slow improvement.  

Experience has also taught me that routines must leave room for improvisation. Equipment is not always reliable, strings snap, and accidents can happen in unexpected places. In Kenya, I drew on my sewing skills to repair a torn tent during a particularly windy night. In Iceland, after my mother fell from a horse in the Southern Highlands, I used a torn T-shirt to splint her leg. A broken string during one of my first jam sessions meant I had to ad lib chords and skip some notes. These moments strengthened my ability to remain calm, take advantage of available resources, and use familiar skills to solve unfamiliar problems.

I’ve found that adapting sometimes also requires slowing down. When learning a new song on guitar, I start at 50% speed, accepting mistakes as part of my progress. I take the same approach to new cultures: I ask people to repeat unfamiliar words, observe before making assumptions, and give relationships time to develop. In Auckland I visited the tourist sites, but I also had my bird-watching spots, a favorite graffiti-mural, and shopkeepers whose morning greetings I looked forward to every day. Slowing down can help contextualize experiences and aid in that important transition from visitor to community member.

In Grenoble, discipline will help me meet the demands of my program, while improvisation, patience, and curiosity will allow me to create a meaningful life within unfamiliar surroundings.

The Statement of Grant Purpose outlines the Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How of what you are proposing for your Fulbright grant. If you are pursuing a research project, developing a strong, feasible and compelling project is the most important aspect of a successful Fulbright application. The Statement of Grant Purpose is limited to 6,000 characters in length including spaces and punctuation.

The first step is to thoroughly read the award description for your selected award and host country to understand the type of grant you are allowed to propose (Independent Study/Research or Graduate Degree Enrollment). Whether you are applying for a research project or a degree program, the proposal should indicate a clear commitment to the host country community and a description of how you will engage with it.

Develop an intellectually compelling and feasible project: This is the most important factor in presenting a successful application.
