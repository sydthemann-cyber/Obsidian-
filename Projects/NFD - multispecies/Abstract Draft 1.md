Working title: Negative Frequency Dependence in Multispecies Marine Biofilms 
- Other titles:
- Temperate Phage Propagation Under Spatial Constraint in Multispecies Biofilms (more similar to peng 2026)

Abstract <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">200 word limit</mark>

Bacteriophages, viruses that infect bacteria, strongly influence bacterial competition, evolution, and pathogenicity. These interactions often occur within biofilms, heterogeneously structured microbial communities that are widespread in natural environments; they also contribute to antibiotic-resistant infections in humans. Previous research in the Nadell Lab using two strains of _Vibrio cholerae_ found that bacteria carrying a temperate phage, called lysogens, experience negative frequency-dependent selection when competing with phage-susceptible bacteria in biofilms. Lysogens benefit at lower frequencies because released phages kill susceptible competitors and allow further chitin colonization by the lysogens. At higher frequencies, lysogenic cells, which have superinfection immunity, adsorb released phages. Consequently, lysogens incur the fitness cost of phage production without receiving the fitness benefit of infecting new cells. Natural biofilms contain multiple bacterial species, including some that are resistant to a given phage. This project will test whether negative frequency-dependent selection persists in a multispecies community containing lysogenic and susceptible strains of _V. cholerae_ together with phage-resistant _Aeromonas_. Fluorescently labeled bacteria will be grown in a microfluidic device that mimics the conditions of marine snow, and confocal fluorescence microscopy will be used to collect data for quantitative image analysis. This work will expand fundamental knowledge of phage-bacteria dynamics and may contribute to a future publication.
