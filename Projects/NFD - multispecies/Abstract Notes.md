##### Abstract: general guidelines

The purpose of an abstract is convey the essential aspects of your project. The abstract should be a **_single paragraph_** of 100-250 words, written in your own words (NOT cut and pasted from your advisor's research abstract), and worded in such a way that it is comprehensible to a general audience.

_What to include in your abstract:_

- Research topic:  begin with a clear statement of the topic and concise description of your project. Is the goal to experiment with color in watercolor paintings? Identify the neural correlates of a cognitive process? Analyze how the media influences political processes?
- Background:  this will vary according to the type of project but should be very brief -- just enough to put the project into context.  It may include such things as previous findings in the field, your own academic work on the topic, consideration of the wider context of the issue, the importance of the topic, etc.
- Methods:  briefly summarize the methodological approach for the project. Will it be a survey? Archival research? Lab experiments? Do NOT include details such as number of subjects, statistical approaches, etc.
- Conclusion:  comment on the anticipated outcome and/or how the findings from the project might contribute to the academic field

##### Abstract: focus on the research

State the purpose of the research itself, not your own plans or your experience with the research or the specific Undergraduate Research program. You should also focus on the research you will be doing and your role in the project, not on your faculty research mentor. For example, your faculty mentor may be planning to write a book or article or hold a symposium, but that is not what you are doing. You are doing research on a particular topic, and your abstract should focus on what the research is about and what you will be doing on the project.
